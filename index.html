Assassin's Creed Valhalla

Become Eivor, a mighty Viking raider, and lead your clan from the harsh shores of Norway to a new home amid the lush farmlands of ninth-century England.

ℹ️ In this game, you have a big, beautiful, mysterious open world where you'll face brutal enemies, raid fortresses, build your clan's new settlement, and forge alliances to win glory and earn a place in Valhalla.

👾 I started working on combat for boss fights, with Goneril (one of the Daughters of Lerion) and her doppelgangers.
I moved then to the Animus Anomalies (which wasn't the name until the very end), where I created the Hazardous Objects. These made me wrestle with physics detection (with all the bugs that come with it, of course), but also taught me about providing proper debug tools to others, in order to accurately pinpoint what is failing, when something fails.
I also was responsible for the Rotatable Splitters, which internally led to a big refactor for pushable objects, and the Draggables, with some fancy options like locking the axis you wanted the item to move on.
And every GPI eventually involves working on its Tutorials, which I was also part of (even for the GPIs that I did not create).
I also did the whole checkpoint system for the anomalies -- when you die in there, you have to respawn at a safe point, instead of loading a previous savegame.
During the massive bugfixing phase by the end of the project, I touched a bit of everything, including combat. Back to boss fights with Regan, the Builder, and the zealots.

💡 Worth mentioning that this game was finished and released during the 2020 pandemic, with everyone working fully remote.
Proudest memory I have from this is fixing a bug that had been present since AC2, and that was pretty annoying to me in Origins.

Assassin's Creed III Remastered

Relive the American Revolution or experience it for the first time in Assassin's Creed III Remastered, with enhanced graphics and improved gameplay mechanics.

ℹ️ AC3 puts you in the boots of Connor (Ratonhnhaké:ton if you are picky), a half-British, half-Mohawk Assassin who fights to protect his people during the American Revolutionary War.
He navigates the complex politics of the revolution while dismantling the Colonial Rite of the Templar Order.

👾 I started working at the end of the project, so it was mainly bugfixing.
Nonetheless, I added various accessibility features regarding the game subtitles (size, color, background), which also led me to play with the Options interface.
I also handled the Graphics menus, with the newly added HDR option that was present in the game.
I also did the 'Track Collectibles' feature, which was a quality of life improvement for the players.
And I put a lot of effort into making sure each subtitle had a speaker name, and that it was the right one -- a feature that came with Origins, and that we had to adapt from scratch for the Remaster.
Well, turns out maybe it was not mainly bugfixing :)

💡 Funny thing I learned here: In Japan, PlayStation has the 'X' and 'O' buttons inverted (the functionality, I mean, controllers look the same).

"Hero"

Face evil armies and become a hero.

ℹ️ Hero (also Warrior Legend) was a third-person action RPG in which players controlled an adventurer fated to save the world.
It was designed for mobile and one-handed play, so it had a vertical layout where the bottom part of your phone was meant for the player's thumb, to drag and tap in order to move the hero and attack.

👾 We put a lot of effort into it, adding layers and layers of systems and meta-gameplay.
Among them, you had things like the Equipment system, with a lot of gear, each item with its stats and rarities. Or the AI companion system, intended to have a bot fighting alongside you, but never overpowering you or getting in your way.
Or the social map, where we simulated a living world with other heroes coming and going, with a hybrid approach of getting the other heroes' data from your actual connected friends, game users, and made-up characters.
At some point we had a hiring system to use a friend's hero to aid you, and we even managed to create the 3 heroes to play with (the Warrior, the Mage, the Archer).

I was made lead of one of the subteams that worked on the project (map + world if I recall correctly), so I handled the subteam/producer communications.
Regarding programming, I handled combat, levels, the map, the social part (friends and living world) and some UI systems.
Outside all of these, since the game was in early stages I also drove multiple gameplay improvement proposals.

💡 Unfortunately, the game was discontinued and ended up being soft-launched in only some specific markets.

Papa Pear Saga

Papa Pear Saga is a colorful, physics-based puzzle game developed by King, known for its lively animations, bouncy gameplay, and quirky humor.
Players launch Papa Pears (cartoonish, bouncing balls) from the top of the screen to hit pegs, objects, and land in buckets.

ℹ️ Papa Pear was a Flash game on king.com's old website (not available anymore), and we were tasked to convert it into a full Facebook game.
We added the 'Saga' format to it, making lots and lots of levels, distributed across thematic chapters.

I was a core team member from start to launch, and I also helped out on the mobile version for a while.

👾 Every time a new element was introduced in a new chapter, it needed specific gameplay. A lot of that was done by me; I recall, for instance, the peas, the autumn leaves or the piranha plant.
Also guilty of adding in-game achievements, which acted more as "action rewards", to boost the player's score by doing specific things like hitting the ceiling, hitting both walls with the same Papa Pear, or shooting 7 Papas straight to a bucket (this created a rainbow peg in a random position, which could automatically light up a bucket).

Besides all things gameplay-related, I also was very invested in the internal level editor.
I added a ton of new stuff there!
Of course, creating new buttons to add the ever-increasing new elements to the levels, but also lots of quality of life improvements for the editor users, like the "drag to place elements in an automatic grid" feature, rather than having to put the elements one by one.

💡 Sadly, King shut down Papa Pear Saga on January 14, 2022.
But of course, Internet exists, which means that nothing really remains dead dead.

Galaxy Life

The funniest massively multiplayer online space strategy game on smartphones, tablets and Facebook!

ℹ️ Galaxy Life is best described as "Clash of Clans, ahead of its time".
You control the Starlings, a cute and clumsy alien civilization that needs your help to rebuild its empire.

Game loop was split into working on your planet and combating other users.
· Working on your planet implied constructing buildings like silos, academies or walls, among others; or repairing them, if damaged. Each building was aimed at one thing (storage capacity, military capacity, defense), and the intention was to always "advance to the next level" and protect your planet, since you could get attacked while you were offline.
· Combat implied space exploration and troop deployment. You could attack offline players and try to steal their resources. Each attacking unit had its own behavior, so you could (more or less) predict the outcome of a battle.

Game started small and ended up really big.

I consider it my first major shipped title, and I was there from conception until launch (on Facebook). Later on, I contributed to Galaxy Life: Pocket Adventures, the mobile port of the game.

👾 I was present in everything involving gameplay at a general level, and in particular the bunkers (troop storage system for battle) and battle AI.
Each unit had its own logic and behavior (e.g. attack the closest building) in the battlefield, and it was the player who decided how, where and when to deploy them.

💡 Fun fact: This game was popular enough that even after closure, it was re-picked up and nowadays you can still find it on the Internet.

Epic Fighters

Defeat the Tyrant King and his horde of epic creatures. An adventure awaits!

ℹ️ Epic Fighters was a web-based dark fantasy RPG game for Facebook, circa 2010.

The user could select a race, from 4 available, to create an "Epic Fighter", and then perform different types of actions that used Stamina.
It had fights and boss fights.
· Fights were a pokémon-like combat system where the user selected Skills with the intention to defeat the opponent.
· Boss fights were social events where you fought an impossibly-high-healthbar enemy, and you could call upon friends and together spend your energies in order to defeat it.

The game was based on a refillable-over-time energy system, and a classical play with friends gimmick from the early Facebook era.

👾 I didn't specifically own anything, since the game was a reskin of a previous title (MMA Pro Fighter) with minimal new things, like the boss fights.

Tons of bugfixing and a good hands-on learning phase for someone who was starting in the industry.

Zezen

Ancient God SETH wants to test your loyalty.
You are ZEZEN, a minotaur and Seth's apprentice. With the help of a giant hammer, prove your worthiness by surviving a temple full of dangers.

In Zezen you found a simple-but-fun action game divided into sections where you battled enemies.
It was a God of War lookalike happening in Egypt with a big minotaur as a protagonist, Zezen, who gave the game its title.
Zezen had a full list of combo attacks, plenty of enemies, and even a big final boss.
Achievements, cheats, splattered blood, ragdoll animation for dead enemies... You name it.

This was my project while doing the Master in Videogame Creation at UPF.
We were the "Basque-Catalan" group, formed by three programmers and three artists, and the game was done in one year, from scratch.
We organized from the start and assigned our areas of work, so we didn't overlap.
My work was diverse. I took care of:
· Main character (combos, animations, sounds)
· Final Boss (behavior, animations, sounds)
· Game Input (keyboard, gamepad, debug console)
· Game/OS communication
· Animations manager, integration with Cal3D
· Sound manager, integration with BASS
· UI
· Achievements
· Tutorials
· Voice of the harpies

We polished the game as much as we could, and we even printed professional-looking DVD cases, with their instruction booklets and everything.
We gifted one to Tim Schafer in ArtFutura 2009, and he made us sign it for him!

We won the Best Presentation award in Barcelona's ArtFutura 2009.

Oh, an ending note: Zezen means 'bull' in Basque :)
